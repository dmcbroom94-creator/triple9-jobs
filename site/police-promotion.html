Police promotion

Sergeant and inspector exams, the competency framework for each rank, forces recruiting on promotion, and the best revision help.

How promotion works (England & Wales)

Most forces use the National Police Promotion Framework (NPPF) for sergeant and inspector. It has four steps:

Step 1EligibilityYour line manager supports you and your PDR shows you are ready.
Step 2Legal examA national multiple-choice law exam run by the College of Policing.
Step 3SelectionYour force’s own board, usually an interview and/or presentation scored against the CVF.
Step 4Temporary promotionYou work in the rank and complete a work-based assessment before becoming substantive.

Police Scotland and PSNI run their own promotion processes.

The framework, rank by rank

What you are assessed on: the CVF

Promotion boards in England and Wales score you against the College of Policing Competency and Values Framework (2024). It has six competencies and three values, each at three levels. Pick your target rank:

Sergeant: usually assessed at Level 2
Supervising a team day to day: setting standards, supporting people and making operational decisions.

We are emotionally aware You notice how your team is feeling, adapt your approach and handle conflict well. Evidence idea: Spotting a struggling officer and getting them support.
We take ownership You take responsibility for your team’s results and fix problems rather than passing them on. Evidence idea: Owning a recurring failure on your shift and putting it right.
We collaborate You build working relationships with partners and other teams to get things done. Evidence idea: Working with a council or school to solve a local problem.
We support and inspire You motivate, coach and develop your team and set a clear example. Evidence idea: Tutoring a new officer through a difficult case.
We analyse critically You weigh up information and risk before deciding, and can explain why. Evidence idea: Making a sound decision at a critical incident with limited information.
We are innovative and open-minded You try new ways of working and learn from feedback. Evidence idea: Introducing a better briefing or handover process.
Values: Courage Respect and empathy Public service

Summaries in plain English. Always read the full framework and the professional profile for your target role on the College of Policing website.

Tips that work

Passing the legal exam

  • Start 5–6 months before the exam. Little and often beats cramming.
  • Use the current year’s Blackstone’s Police Manuals. They are the official syllabus the questions are written from.
  • Do lots of practice questions. Learn why wrong answers are wrong.
  • Sit at least one full timed mock exam before the real thing.
  • Join or start a study group. Explaining law to others makes it stick.
  • Book leave for the last week or two if you can.

Getting through the board

  • Keep an evidence log all year: one STAR example for each CVF competency at your target level.
  • Choose recent examples where you led or made the difference, not the team.
  • Research the force’s priorities and Police and Crime Plan for presentation questions.
  • Practise out loud with a supervisor who has sat on boards.
  • Show the values too: courage, respect and empathy, public service.
  • Prepare a lesson learned for every example. Follow-up questions are common.

Temporary promotion & assessment

  • Ask for an acting-up opportunity before your board. It gives you better examples.
  • Agree your work-based assessment evidence early with your assessor.
  • Keep notes of decisions you make and why. They become your evidence.
  • Find a mentor one rank above your target.
