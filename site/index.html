Questions people ask

How do police transfers work?

Transferee officers move from one force to another, usually keeping their rank, pay point and pension. Most forces ask that you have finished your probation, are substantive in rank and have no live misconduct or performance matters. Each force sets its own process, often an application, an interview and vetting.

What is a police rejoiner?

A rejoiner is a former officer coming back to policing after a break. Rules on how long you can have been out, and which rank you return at, vary by force, so check each advert.

Are police staff jobs included?

Yes. We list police staff roles such as civilian investigators, CSI and forensics, detention and custody officers, 999 call handlers, analysts and admin roles, as well as officer jobs.

I want to leave the emergency services. What jobs suit me?

Your skills fit many civilian careers, including fraud and investigation, security and risk, health and safety, emergency planning, training, operations management and customer service. Use our free CV builder to turn your service experience into a civilian CV.

Do I need to sign up?

No. There are no accounts and we do not store your details. Every job links straight to the employer’s official advert, and our tools work on your own device.

Which areas do you cover?

Jobs across England, Scotland and Wales, including Home Office forces, Police Scotland, British Transport Police, the Civil Nuclear Constabulary, MOD Police, port police, prisons, Border Force and the ambulance and fire services.
