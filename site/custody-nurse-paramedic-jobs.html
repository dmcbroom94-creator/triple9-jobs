What the job involves

Custody healthcare professionals look after people detained in police custody. Typical duties in the adverts we list include:

  • assessing whether detainees are fit to be detained, interviewed or sent to court
  • physical and mental health assessments, minor injuries and substance withdrawal
  • collecting forensic samples and writing statements
  • advising custody sergeants and staff on risk and welfare

Who can apply

  • Registered nurses (adult, mental health or children’s) with the NMC, or paramedics registered with the HCPC.
  • Most employers ask for around two years’ post-qualification experience in a setting such as A&E, urgent care, the ambulance service or prison healthcare.
  • You’ll need to pass police vetting, and many roles ask for a driving licence.

Full forensic training is usually given, so you don’t need custody experience to start.

Who recruits

Custody healthcare is run either by the police force itself (the Met employs its own Custody Healthcare Practitioners) or by contracted providers such as Mitie Care & Custody, SONAR Cares and Mountain Healthcare. Recent adverts on this page have offered roughly £41,000 to £49,000 a year for full-time posts, with overtime, shift work and registration fees often paid.
